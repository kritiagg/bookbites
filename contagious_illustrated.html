Contagious

Jonah Berger
Why things catch on — and how a comedy creator can engineer word-of-mouth on purpose instead of praying to the algorithm. The science of why we share, packaged for Instagram & YouTube growth.
THE SIX FORCES OF SHARING · S·T·E·P·P·S
1

Word-of-Mouth Beats Ads (Your Audience Is Your Ad Budget)

People trust a friend's recommendation over your perfectly-lit promo reel.
★ KEY LESSON

Berger opens by busting the myth that virality is luck or a giant ad budget. Most everyday spread of ideas happens face-to-face and in feeds — trusted, targeted, and free. Ads shout at strangers; word-of-mouth is a friend leaning in. The book's whole thesis: shareability is designed, not gifted. You can build the six STEPPS triggers into your content on purpose.

  • Blendtec spent almost nothing on ads — its "Will It Blend?" videos drove a reported ~700% sales jump purely through sharing.
  • Please Don't Tell, a hidden NYC bar entered through a phone booth in a hot-dog shop, became one of the toughest reservations in the city without advertising — people couldn't stop whispering about it.
  • Berger's research found the content people share most isn't the best-funded — it's the most share-WORTHY (it makes the sharer look good, feel something, or help someone).
😅 FUNNY TAKE

You can boost a post for $50 and reach 4,000 strangers who scroll past it while on the toilet. Or you can make one video so good your aunt shares it to a group chat called "Recipes & Drama." Berger's entire book is a 200-page way of saying: be the group-chat post, not the toilet ad.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Word-of-mouth is network propagation — the same math as an SIR epidemic model or influence-maximization on a graph. Paid ads are a flat broadcast; sharing is a branching cascade where one "infected" node seeds many. The recommender system (TikTok/YT) is just an amplifier on top: it observes early engagement signals and decides whether to fan your cascade out across the graph. Engineer share-worthiness and you're improving your reproduction number R, not buying impressions.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

The Tipping Point (Gladwell): connectors and "stickiness" spreading socially. Hook Point (Kane): you still need a 3-second hook before word-of-mouth can carry anything. Influence (Cialdini): social proof is the engine under "my friend recommended it."

"Word of mouth is the primary factor behind 20 to 50 percent of all purchasing decisions."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
2

Social Currency — Make People Look Smart for Sharing You

We share things that make US look good. Be the post that earns the sharer clout.
★ KEY LESSON

People share things that make them look in-the-know, clever, or part of an exclusive club. Berger calls this social currency. The trick: find your content's "inner remarkability," use scarcity/exclusivity to make viewers feel like insiders, and build in game mechanics that let people show off status.

  • Please Don't Tell: the bar's whole brand is secrecy — sharing it makes YOU the person with insider knowledge. Counterintuitively, "don't tell" makes people tell.
  • Inner remarkability: Blendtec turned a boring blender into a flex — "I follow the channel that blends iPhones" is social currency.
  • Game mechanics / status: airline elite tiers and Foursquare "mayor" badges work because visible status is currency people show off.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

Nobody reshares your reel because they love you. They reshare it because it makes THEM look like they have a refined sense of humor. Your job as a comedy creator is to be the joke smart people repost to prove they're smart. You're not the comedian — you're their personality.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Social currency is a reward signal in the sharer's utility function, not yours. A recommender optimizes "will this user share/save/send?" — and the latent feature driving that is "does sharing raise my perceived status?" Think of an embedding of identity signaling: content sits near a user's aspirational self-image in vector space and they reshare to project it. Design content whose embedding lands close to "things impressive people post."

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Influence (Cialdini): scarcity and exclusivity as persuasion levers. Show Your Work! (Kleon): sharing process makes your audience feel like insiders to your craft. Hook Point: a remarkable, status-worthy angle is itself a hook.

"We share things that make us look good. Just as people use money to buy products or services, they use social currency to achieve desired positive impressions among their families, friends, and colleagues."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
3

Triggers — Top of Mind, Tip of Tongue

Link your content to a frequent cue in everyday life and you get free reminders forever.
★ KEY LESSON

Triggers are stimuli in the environment that make people think of your thing. The more often the trigger occurs, the more often people think of you. Berger's rule: don't chase the catchiest message — chase the one welded to a frequent, relevant cue.

  • Kit Kat + coffee: In 2007 brand manager Colleen Chorak tied Kit Kat to coffee breaks ("a break's best friend"). Coffee is everywhere, so the candy got triggered constantly — sales reportedly grew dramatically over the campaign.
  • Rebecca Black's "Friday": the song spikes in views every Friday because the day itself is the trigger. Free weekly reminder, baked into the calendar.
  • Mars bars: sales reportedly ticked up when NASA's Mars mission was in the news — people heard "Mars" and the candy bar surfaced.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

"Friday" is a mediocre song that became immortal because it shackled itself to a day that happens every single week. That's the dream: make a "Sunday Scaries" bit so on-the-nose that every Sunday night, 8pm, your audience involuntarily thinks of you while staring at their unopened work laptop.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

A trigger is a retrieval cue — the query that pulls your memory out of storage. In ML terms you're maximizing recall frequency: bind your content's embedding to a high-base-rate context vector (Monday, coffee, gym, payday) so that everyday inputs surface you. It's also a reinforcement schedule: a recurring environmental cue acts like a periodic reward prompt that keeps re-activating the memory trace before it decays.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Hook Point: a recurring format is a self-trigger ("oh it's the Tuesday series"). Made to Stick (Heath bros): concrete, simple ideas are easier for a trigger to retrieve. Influence: consistency — repeated cues build habitual association.

"Top of mind means tip of tongue. People often talk about whatever comes to mind, so the more often people think about a product or idea, the more it will be talked about."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
4

Emotion — When We Care, We Share

Strong feelings are the fuel. Make people feel something big, not nothing nice.
★ KEY LESSON

Berger and Milkman's research found it's not positive-vs-negative that drives sharing — it's arousal. High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety, excitement, amusement) fire people up and get content passed on. Low-arousal states (sadness, contentment) make people relax and do nothing. So a heartwarming-but-mild video underperforms an awe-inspiring or hilarious one.

  • Awe drives shares: Berger's New York Times study found awe-inspiring science and big-idea pieces hit the most-emailed list far more than mundane news.
  • Amusement: funny content is high-arousal positive — exactly the lane a comedy creator lives in. Your superpower is literally STEPPS' "E."
  • Anger & anxiety spread too (think outrage threads) — high arousal, just the riskier kind to build a brand on.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

Science says people share when they're amused, awed, or furious. So your content menu is basically: make 'em laugh, make 'em gasp, or make 'em type "this is the problem with society today" in the comments. A calm, pleasant video where everyone is mildly content is the algorithmic equivalent of a lullaby. Congrats, you've put 40,000 people to sleep.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Arousal is the physiological correlate of a large reward-prediction error — a spike that flags "this is worth acting on." Recommenders implicitly learn this: comments, rapid replays, and shares are high-signal reinforcement the model weights heavily, while a passive full-watch on calm content is weak signal. Note the reward-hacking trap: anger/anxiety maximize short-term arousal metrics but can degrade your long-term retention reward — the model gives you a hit, then the audience burns out.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Made to Stick: the "Emotional" in their SUCCESs framework — make people feel, not just understand. Hook Point: emotional jolt in the first 3 seconds. Born Standing Up (Steve Martin): amusement and tension/release as a craft you can engineer.

"When we care, we share. Rather than focusing on function, we need to focus on feelings."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
5

Public — Built to Show, Built to Grow

If people can't see others doing it, they can't copy it. Make behavior observable.
★ KEY LESSON

People imitate what they can observe — "monkey see, monkey do." Berger says we should make the private public and design things that advertise themselves. Behavioral residue (visible leftovers of using a product) keeps spreading the signal after the moment passes.

  • Apple's white earbuds: in a sea of black headphones, white cords made iPod users visible to everyone — turning every commuter into a walking ad and triggering imitation.
  • Movember: growing a mustache makes a private donation to men's-health causes publicly observable, so the behavior recruits more participants on sight.
  • Behavioral residue: tote bags, "Sent from my iPhone," livestream sub-counts — leftovers that keep showing the world the choice was made.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

This is why every creator screenshots their own milestones. "Just hit 100k!!" isn't humility, it's behavioral residue — a public mustache for your follower count. Make your audience want to wear your brand like white earbuds: a catchphrase, a duet format, a tag they slap on their own posts so the algorithm sees the same cult forming in real time.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Observability is what makes social proof a self-reinforcing feedback loop. Visible adoption raises a node's prior that adopting is correct — preferential attachment, the rich-get-richer dynamic behind viral cascades. For recommenders, public engagement counts (likes, shares, duet trees) are the features the model reads as quality. Designing shareable artifacts (a duet template, a sound, a hashtag) literally manufactures the training signal that gets you distributed.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Influence: social proof — we look to others to decide what's right. The Tipping Point: visible adoption tips a trend. Show Your Work!: making your process public is the creator version of behavioral residue.

"If something is built to show, it's built to grow."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
6

Practical Value — News You Can Use

People share useful things to help others (and to look helpful doing it).
★ KEY LESSON

We share useful info because helping others is social glue — it shows we care and that we're competent. Berger's advice: package practical value so it's easy to spot, easy to use, and easy to pass along. Highlight remarkable savings, narrow your audience so it feels "for them," and make the tip obviously actionable.

  • Ken Craig's corn video: a grandfather's microwave-and-shuck hack for silk-free corn racked up over 5 million views — pure usefulness people forwarded to family.
  • How-to & listicles: Berger's research showed practically useful articles ("how to," guides) were reliably among the most-emailed — utility travels.
  • Narrow it: a tip framed for a specific group ("for new parents," "for broke comedians") feels more shareable to that group than a generic one.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

A retired man microwaving corn out-performed most marketing budgets in history because he solved an actual problem. Meanwhile your 11-part skit series got 300 views. The lesson: sneak a genuinely useful tip into your comedy and watch people share it "for the hack" while accidentally giving you a career. Edutainment is just a Trojan horse with better lighting.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Practical value is high information gain — content that measurably reduces a viewer's uncertainty about a task. Recommenders detect it through save/bookmark and rewatch signals (the "I need this later" reward), which weight differently than a quick like. It's also why retrieval-augmented how-to content compounds: useful videos get re-surfaced by search-intent queries long after posting — a long-tail reward stream, not a one-day spike.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Made to Stick: concrete, actionable info sticks. Show Your Work!: teach what you know — utility builds an audience that returns. Hook Point: "here's how to ___ in 30 seconds" is both a hook and practical value.

"People like to pass along practical, useful information. News others can use."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
7

Stories — The Trojan Horse That Carries Your Brand

People don't share information; they share narratives. Make the message ride inside the story.
★ KEY LESSON

Information travels under the cover of story — we don't memorize facts, we remember tales and retell them. Berger's key warning: build a "valuable virality." The brand benefit must be essential to the narrative, or people share the story and forget you. If the moral can survive without your product, your product gets edited out.

  • Subway & Jared Fogle: the "guy who lost a huge amount of weight eating Subway" story spread because the weight loss required Subway — the brand was load-bearing, not a footnote.
  • Trojan horse principle: wrap your point inside an emotional, retellable story so people carry your message without feeling marketed to.
  • Make it integral: if you can tell the joke/story and leave your channel name out without losing anything, the story isn't doing your marketing for you.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

The terrifying lesson for creators: people will absolutely retell your funniest bit at brunch — and credit it to "this video I saw," not you. That's a story without a load-bearing brand. Build your name INTO the punchline so when they retell it, they have to say "you know, the [your handle] guy." Otherwise you're just a ghostwriter for strangers' personalities.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Stories are lossy compression with high fidelity on the parts that matter — humans transmit narrative far better than raw facts because the schema aids encoding and recall. "Valuable virality" is an attribution problem: like ensuring your brand is a necessary feature, not a spurious one a model would prune. If the story's reward is achievable with your brand zeroed out, gradient updates (retellings) will drop it. Make the brand a non-removable token in the sequence.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Made to Stick: Stories are the final "S" in SUCCESs. Born Standing Up: Steve Martin's persona was the through-line of every bit. Show Your Work!: tell the story of how things get made; Hook Point: a story-shaped hook out-pulls a statement.

"People don't think in terms of information. They think in terms of narratives. Make your message so integral to the narrative that people can't tell the story without it."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
8

High vs Low Arousal — Engineer the Spike

Pick emotions that make people physically want to move. Calm content dies quiet.
★ KEY LESSON

This is the engineering corollary of Lesson 4: don't just "be emotional," be activating. Berger & Milkman showed arousal — the bodily fired-up feeling — predicts sharing better than whether the emotion is good or bad. Want shares? Stir awe, amusement, excitement, anger, or anxiety. Avoid drifting into the cozy, content middle.

  • Awe outperforms cute: a jaw-dropping reveal travels; a pleasant "aw, nice" video stalls. Build a "wait, WHAT" moment.
  • Add a physiological kick: Berger notes even unrelated arousal (exercise, suspense) boosts sharing — pace, music, and tension raise the meter.
  • Comedy = high-arousal positive: the safest sharable lane. Surprise + amusement is exactly what a creator should chase over "wholesome and quiet."
😅 FUNNY TAKE

There's a reason "rage bait" exists and "mild-contentment bait" does not. Nobody has ever frantically tagged six friends under a video captioned "this made me feel pleasantly okay." You want the audience's heart rate up. Make them laugh hard enough to wake their roommate. That involuntary "send" reflex is the entire business model.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Arousal ≈ gradient magnitude on the share decision: high-arousal content produces a steep update toward "act," low-arousal a flat one toward "do nothing." Engagement-based recommenders are effectively arousal classifiers — they reward rapid, intense reactions. Caution: optimizing pure arousal is classic reward hacking — outrage farms the metric while eroding trust (the true long-horizon objective). The aligned move is high-arousal-positive (awe, amusement) that's sustainable.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Hook Point: the 3-second jolt is an arousal spike. Born Standing Up: Steve Martin built tension with no punchline release — pure managed arousal. Made to Stick: "Unexpected" generates the surprise that drives arousal.

"Physiological arousal or activation drives people to talk and share. Some emotions kindle the fire; others put it out."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
9

Designing Triggers on Purpose — Build the Habit Loop

Don't hope a cue appears. Manufacture a recurring one and chain your content to it.
★ KEY LESSON

The most overlooked tactic in the book: you can create triggers, not just find them. Tie your content to a cue that recurs often in your audience's day, and the world reminds them of you for free. The frequency of the trigger matters more than how clever the link is.

  • Recurring formats: a "Monday Meltdown" or "Friday Recap" series turns the calendar into your alarm clock — the day cues the audience, like "Friday" cued Rebecca Black.
  • Borrow ambient cues: Kit Kat hitched to coffee because coffee is everywhere; pick a daily object/moment your niche encounters constantly.
  • Habit chaining: "watch this with your morning coffee" links your drop to an existing habit so the habit pulls the content.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

Pick a posting schedule so consistent that your audience develops a Pavlovian twitch. The goal is for someone to spill coffee on a Monday and think "ugh, I haven't watched the bit yet" before they think about their actual job. You're not building a channel, you're building a low-grade addiction with a content calendar.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Designing triggers is engineering the cue → behavior → reward loop — the same structure as a policy learned by reinforcement. A fixed schedule is a predictable state feature the audience's "policy" can condition on; consistency lowers their search cost (they don't have to explore to find you). Recommenders amplify this: regular cadence creates stable engagement priors, so the system learns to pre-fetch your content to known fans at the cued time.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Hook Point: a signature recurring format is itself a hook and a trigger. Influence: consistency and commitment build the habit. The Tipping Point: small, sticky repeated cues accumulate into a trend.

"The best triggers are ones where there is a strong link, and where the trigger occurs frequently. Grow the habitat — find frequent stimuli and link your idea to them."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
10

Inner Remarkability — Find the Thing Worth Talking About

Boring products and channels don't spread. Surface the surprising, extraordinary angle.
★ KEY LESSON

Berger argues nothing is inherently boring — you find its inner remarkability: the surprising, novel, or extreme angle that violates expectations. Remarkable things are, literally, worth remarking on — they hand people social currency. The job is to ask "what's the most extreme/unexpected truth about this?"

  • Blendtec "Will It Blend?": a humble blender became must-watch by blending an iPhone, marbles, a rake handle — remarkability manufactured from a dull product.
  • Find the extreme: the $100 cheesesteak (Barclay Prime in Philadelphia) made a sandwich newsworthy; extremity creates conversation.
  • Creator version: "I did [normal thing] in the most insane way possible" — the format MrBeast-style channels live on is engineered remarkability.
😅 FUNNY TAKE

A man destroyed iPhones in a blender and built an empire, while you sit on a genuinely funny life and post nothing because "it's not interesting enough." Everything is interesting at the right angle and the right intensity. Your weird family, your worst job, your unhinged take on cereal — that's an iPhone waiting for a blender.

🤖 AI / ML LENS

Remarkability is high surprisal — low-probability content under the viewer's prior, which is exactly what grabs attention (think novelty bonuses in exploration). Recommenders reward the outlier engagement signature a surprising video produces: unusual watch-time and comment spikes flag it as worth exploring beyond your followers. In embedding space, you want content far from the dense, average cluster — distinctive vectors get explored; generic ones get ignored.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Made to Stick: "Unexpected" breaks the pattern and earns attention. Hook Point: a remarkable angle is the hook. Show Your Work!: the surprising detail of your process is what's worth remarking on.

"Remarkable things provide social currency because they make the people who talk about them seem, well, more remarkable."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
11

STEPPS Together — It's a Recipe, Not a Menu

You don't need all six. Stack two or three deliberately and you've engineered virality.
★ KEY LESSON

Berger is clear: the most contagious things hit several STEPPS at once, but you rarely need all six. Audit your idea against the checklist and strengthen the two or three you can win. As a comedy creator your home turf is Emotion (amusement) and Social Currency — bolt on a Trigger and a public Format and you've built a machine.

  • "Will It Blend?" = Emotion (awe/amusement) + Social Currency (remarkable) + Practical Value (does it actually blend?) stacked.
  • Movember = Public (visible mustache) + Stories (why I'm growing it) + Social Currency (look, I'm doing good).
  • Your move: run every post through the 6-point checklist before publishing — which boxes does it tick, and which can you add in 5 minutes?
😅 FUNNY TAKE

STEPPS is basically a nutrition label for your content. Most creators publish empty calories — pure vibes, zero shareable nutrients — then act shocked when the algorithm doesn't gain weight. Run your post through the six letters like a paranoid parent reading ingredients: "this has Triggers and Emotion but ZERO Practical Value, absolutely not, back in the drafts you go."

🤖 AI / ML LENS

STEPPS is a feature vector for shareability: six engineered features, each a partial predictor of the share label. The contagious hits are points where multiple features fire — like an ensemble where weak learners combine into a strong signal. Practically it's a checklist that maximizes your content's score under the recommender's implicit objective. You're doing manual feature engineering for an optimizer you can't see directly.

🔗 CONNECTS TO

Made to Stick's SUCCESs: a parallel six-letter checklist for stickiness — use both together. Hook Point: your STEPPS-loaded idea still needs a sharp hook to enter. Show Your Work!: a daily-sharing practice gives you reps to test the recipe.

"Six principles of contagiousness: products and ideas that contain Social Currency and are Triggered, Emotional, Public, Practically Valuable, and wrapped into Stories."
— Jonah Berger, Contagious
SPOTLIGHT STORIES · CASE STUDIES THAT CAUGHT ON
Spotlight · Emotion + Social Currency

"Will It Blend?" — How a Blender Became Appointment Viewing

Tom Dickson, Blendtec's founder, had a sturdy blender and a problem: blenders are boring. So he filmed himself in a lab coat blending absurd things — marbles, a rake handle, and most famously an iPhone — under the deadpan tagline "Will It Blend?"

The series found the product's inner remarkability and wrapped it in awe and amusement (high-arousal Emotion). Individual videos pulled tens of millions of views, and Blendtec reported sales rising dramatically — by a widely-cited ~700% — on almost no ad spend.

Creator takeaway: Your niche isn't boring; you haven't found its iPhone-in-a-blender yet. Take the most ordinary thing you do and push it to a remarkable, surprising extreme that people feel compelled to show a friend.

Spotlight · Social Currency + Scarcity

"Please Don't Tell" — The Bar That Grew by Telling You to Shut Up

In New York's East Village, you enter PDT through a vintage phone booth inside a hot-dog shop. There's no sign, no ads — just a secret door and, on your way out, a small card reading "Please Don't Tell."

It became one of the city's hardest reservations precisely because it felt like a secret. Knowing about it is social currency: telling a friend makes you the insider. The "don't tell" framing is reverse psychology — secrets are made to be shared, carefully, with the right people, which is exactly how exclusivity spreads.

Creator takeaway: Build a little exclusivity into your world — an inside joke, a members-only bit, a "if you know, you know" reference. Make your real fans feel like insiders and they'll recruit the next ones to prove they're in the club.

Spotlight · Practical Value

Ken Craig's Corn Hack — When Pure Usefulness Goes Viral

Ken Craig, an older gentleman, filmed a simple kitchen tip: microwave corn in its husk, then shake it out — and the silk slides off, leaving "clean ears every time." No production value, no celebrity, no hook gimmick.

It racked up over 5 million views because it was genuinely, immediately useful. People shared it not to look cool but to help — forwarding it to parents and friends who cook. Practical value is the quiet workhorse of STEPPS: it doesn't need to be flashy, it needs to work.

Creator takeaway: Slip a real, usable nugget into your content. A comedy cooking bit that actually teaches a hack gets forwarded by people who don't even follow you — and useful videos keep getting re-surfaced by search long after the laughs fade.

The STEPPS Cheat Sheet

S

Social Currency

Make sharing you make THEM look good — smart, in-the-know, exclusive. Find your inner remarkability. Ex: Please Don't Tell, Blendtec.

T

Triggers

Link your idea to a frequent everyday cue so the environment reminds people of you. Top of mind = tip of tongue. Ex: Kit Kat + coffee, "Friday."

E

Emotion

When we care, we share. Spark high-arousal feelings — awe, amusement, anger, anxiety, excitement — not calm contentment. Your comedy lane = amusement.

P

Public

Built to show, built to grow. Make behavior observable and leave behavioral residue. Ex: Apple white earbuds, Movember mustaches.

P

Practical Value

News you can use. Make help easy to find, use, and pass on. Narrow the audience so it feels "for them." Ex: Ken Craig's corn hack.

S

Stories

Wrap your message in a Trojan-horse narrative people retell — and make the brand essential so it can't be edited out. Ex: Subway & Jared.

🎯 The Creator's One-Line Playbook

Before you hit post: make it remarkable (Social Currency), chain it to a recurring cue (Triggers), make them laugh or gasp (Emotion / high arousal), give them something to wear or duet (Public), sneak in a real tip (Practical Value), and bake your name into the punchline (Stories). Win two or three and the algorithm does the rest.
